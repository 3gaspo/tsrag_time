\section*{Main findings}
On 90 matched TIME tasks, default TS-RAG has 12.31\% higher arithmetic mean
task MASE than maximum-context Chronos-2. The frozen validation mixture has
0.24\% higher mean MASE. This comparison establishes no aggregate accuracy
benefit for retrieval or mixing. TS-RAG uses maximum-context Chronos-Bolt
fallback on 42.75\% of scored rows. Newly published compact metadata shows
the mixture's validation support, but its aggregate reason counts combine
validation and test and are not eligible evaluation evidence.

\section{Question and performed protocol}
Does retrieval improve a frozen model beyond the effect of context length,
and can validation mixing improve the stronger control?
For observed history $x$ and future $y\in\mathbb R^H$, with horizon $H$,
each method produces a median point forecast $\widehat y$.
The controls are Chronos-T5-base at 512 observations, Chronos-Bolt-base at
512 and 2,048, and Chronos-2 at 8,192. History is capped at these lengths;
controls are univariate without covariates. T5 uses 20 forecast samples.
Seed zero is used once.

Default TS-RAG retrieves ten neighbors using T5 representations across all
items and variates (measured channels) in the same dataset. Complete
512-point histories and 64-point continuations must be observed by the real
query date. The released mixture-of-experts adaptation module emits
64-point chunks. Longer horizons roll forward while neighbor eligibility
stays anchored to the real query date. Date-phase alignment and query-scale
normalization are disabled. Unavailable retrieval or invalid native
forecasts use the independent 2,048-context Bolt forecast for the entire
window/variate.

Validation precedes test. With $w$ TS-RAG wins in $n$ usable paired
validation trials (ties count one half), freeze $p=(1+w)/(2+n)$ before test.
No usable validation gives $p=0$. The mixture is
$(1-p)\widehat y_{\rm Chronos2}+p\widehat y_{\rm TS\text{-}RAG}$.
Test retrieval admits observed validation history under the same causal rule.

\section{Evaluation and accuracy}
MASE divides window mean absolute error by mean absolute seasonal difference
over finite historical pairs in the observed prefix. Task means average
finite item/window/variate values; the table equally averages 90 tasks.
Lower is better. All six methods have finite MASE on the same 67,502 declared
cells; the report selects 540 completed evaluations.

\section{Paired comparisons and fallback}
TS-RAG wins eight tasks and loses 82 against Chronos-2. The mixture wins
36 and loses 54. Against Bolt-max, TS-RAG has 2.82\% higher mean MASE,
with 19 wins, 52 losses and 19 ties. Increasing Bolt's context alone improves
its mean relative to Bolt-512; retrieval does not beat Bolt-max on this mean.

TS-RAG falls back on 28,860 of 67,502 scored rows (42.75\%).
Fallback occurs in 49 tasks: 19 have complete and 30 partial fallback;
41 have none. Full-fallback task means match Bolt-max. Complete coverage
does not establish native TS-RAG success on every row. No task-level errors
are reported.

The published aggregate cause counts combine validation and test, although only
test is evaluated. They are therefore excluded from scientific conclusions.
The corrected artifact contract reports aggregate fallback causes on test alone
while retaining validation masks and per-row reasons as separate mixture
diagnostics. Test-only cause totals require a new run under that contract.

Frozen TS-RAG mixture weights range from 0.0455 to 0.7143, with mean 0.3454.
All 90 weights are estimated from at least one usable validation date.
Validation support totals 27,914 dates, ranges from 1 to 14,868 per task,
and averages 310.16. The wide support range means task weights have very
different estimation precision; the present study does not quantify it.

\section{Cost and evidence limits}
TS-RAG additionally records 55,578.80 seconds of datastore preprocessing.
Its inference total reuses computed Bolt-max fallback forecasts; mixture
totals include both components and mixing. These are recorded pipeline
costs, not independent fresh-process latency. They support no speed benefit.

The completed report's selected manifests, compact MASE means and population
dispersion agree. The inspected publication now contains all 90 preparation,
90 extraction, 540 prediction and 90 mixture-weight records. Aggregate
calibration support extends the mixture diagnosis without changing the accuracy
result; the phase-combined fallback causes do not.

Raw predictions, metric arrays, shared Seasonal masks and per-row reason
arrays have not been independently audited. Test-only cause totals and
native-only accuracy on a common support are not yet reported. One seed
provides no across-run uncertainty or significance
claim. Population variance/std describes finite metric cells within a task.
Conclusions apply only to the default retrieval settings; the implemented
16-cell retrieval ablation has not been executed.
